# Give a landing page a reason to exist

Use this for a public product introduction, campaign or mixed marketing/demo
page. Read with [product identity](product-identity.md). An operating screen can
be excellent at completing a task and still be an incomplete product landing
page. In the OFFBEAT example, the user rejected a working dark audio catalogue
because it lacked a persuasive introduction and a positive visual identity.
Passing playback and rental tests did not resolve that rejection.

## A small music-product comparison

On 2026-11-27, actual Chromium samples of
[Output Arcade](https://output.com/products/arcade) and
[Ableton Live](https://www.ableton.com/en/live/) were inspected at 1440px and
390px, with one lower desktop sample. Arcade's strong headline/action hierarchy
and repeated typographic voice differ from Live's campaign lettering, studio
imagery and editorial composition. These are interpretations of sampled renders,
not claims that either approach is universally better. Transfer the relationship
between the promise, visual and demonstration; leave their lettering, palette,
campaign assets, claims and page sequence with those brands.

Coverage was partial: Ableton's cookie layer was closed before follow-up captures,
some lower Arcade assets were missing, and product interactions and commercial
effectiveness were not assessed. A Splice text fetch was readable but its rendered
sample was blank, so it was not used as visual-quality evidence. Research access
failures do not justify filling the missing page with assumptions.

## Identify the visitor's decision

State what the visitor needs to understand or want before using the tool. Connect
the offer, audience, useful distinction and next step in ordinary language. Keep
the actual product: a software-like presentation of an instrument rental concept
does not turn it into a DAW, a subscription library or a real available service.

Sketch the reading progression as visitor questions, then give each section a
distinct job. A demonstration can make the offer concrete; service details can
resolve uncertainty; a selection or plan can support action. This is not a fixed
hero/features/proof/pricing/FAQ recipe. Do not invent testimonials, usage numbers,
capabilities or urgency to fill a supposed marketing requirement.

## Make art direction carry meaning

Design a few mutually supporting relationships rather than adding a fashionable
skin to the operating UI. Explain how the actual words, letterforms, type scale,
surface proportions, accent use, image framing and controls express this product.
Use a deliberate focal visual when useful: an authored product illustration,
real output or working demonstration can create interest before dense controls.
An image can contribute emotion and recognition without being a button.

Inspect the hero and at least one transition at actual reading size. Check the
optical relationship between headline and visual, the path to a useful action,
and how the identity changes density around details and forms. More sections,
larger type, a dark panel, a new font or a saturated accent do not by themselves
make the result polished. Familiar compositions remain valid when executed well.

Keep demonstrations truthful and proportionate. Show actual behavior or label
illustrative material. A fake recorder, decorative meters, non-working controls
or a screenshot suggesting unavailable features can weaken the product story.
Conversely, do not replace a useful operating browser just to make the hero look
different. A live tool can be the lead experience when the brief and audience
support that choice; it does not need a slogan or a large illustration by rule.

## Recheck the whole visitor experience

Compare the rejected page with the revision at matched desktop and phone widths.
Describe what was created in the offer, focal visual, reading rhythm and action
hierarchy. Inspect a meaningful lower section and the actual useful journey.
Retain normal controls, accurate costs, error recovery and accessibility.

Keep four outcomes separate: functional preservation, visual execution in the
reviewed samples, support for the landing-page decision, and user acceptance.
An agent's interpretation of taste is not an objective score or proof of improved
conversion. A source-only plan leaves these outcomes unassessed. A deliberately
plain documentation site or direct-to-tool product is a legitimate counterexample
to presentation-led advice; preserve an accepted minimal introduction.
