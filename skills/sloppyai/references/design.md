# Design review

Classify the surface by the visitor's goal. Marketing must explain an offer and
support a decision; operational UI must support a task; reading material needs
comprehension and navigation; a showcase should foreground the work.

Inspect first impression, hierarchy, composition, typography, colour roles,
spacing, imagery, consistency and meaningful motion. Name what the eye notices
first and whether that order supports the task. Inspect the actual rendered
surface when possible; source classes alone do not establish visual quality.

Review cards, badges, eyebrows, dividers and repeated sections for information
value. For operational interfaces, identify the job each label, icon, description
and separator performs; remove duplication only when essential meaning remains.
See [operational interfaces](operational-ui.md) for an evidence-based audit. Preserve real statuses, filters, prices and boundaries. Evaluate font and
palette choices against the audience and accepted identity. Gradients, serif
fonts, mono, rounded corners, punctuation and visual polish are legitimate choices.

Compare a proposed repair with the baseline at relevant widths and states. Keep
factual claims and real product evidence visible. Check related components and
tokens rather than making a disconnected local style change.

Use catalogue prefixes B for optional background techniques, D for improvement
heuristics, T for definitions and H for contextual style observations. Their
presence is never an AI-authorship probability or an automatic release failure.
