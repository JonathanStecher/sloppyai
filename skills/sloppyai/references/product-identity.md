# Learn from real products and build an identity

Use this when a website works but still feels generic, including after a pass that
removed decoration. Read alongside [visual quality](visual-quality.md) and
[quality benchmarks](quality-benchmarks.md). The purpose is to create a coherent
identity for this product, not an absence of familiar patterns.

## What was missing from a removal-only review

Simplification can improve clarity without creating any character. A default
sans-serif, a blank background and a literal price list can be as interchangeable
as an ornamental template. Keep the functional gains, then decide what the work
should positively express through its content, imagery, typography, colour,
composition and interactions.

An expressive choice can earn its place through personality, emotional tone or a
recognisable brand relationship. It does not need to operate a control. The test
is whether the combined choices serve this product and are executed well. Do not
turn a purpose audit into an instruction to remove all joy, illustration or craft.

## Study a reference as a system

Select a small, contrasting set: an adjacent product, a different category with
relevant execution, and a legitimate counterexample. Inspect the real rendered
pages and one useful interaction at wide and narrow widths when available. Name
which parts were seen; a text fetch alone cannot establish their visual quality.

For each source, record **observation → relationship → transferable principle →
what must not transfer**. Ask how its content, assets, type, palette, composition
and behaviour reinforce each other. Do not collect screenshots as a shopping list
of hero shapes, colour codes and fonts. Treat access failures and obscured pages
as partial evidence rather than filling in the missing pixels.

## Build a positive brief for the actual product

Start from a product truth or tension: who needs this, what do they care about,
what work is real, and what could this product say that a different one could not?
Then propose a concise identity with visible consequences. "Premium", "friendly"
or "less AI" alone still leaves the implementation at its defaults.

Connect a few mutually supporting choices:

- **Voice and content:** an offer, useful distinctions and a tone appropriate to
  this audience. Do not invent customers, credentials or proof to make it feel real.
- **Type:** character in real words, optical spacing and a hierarchy that continues
  into prices, forms and feedback. A single well-used family can suffice. Licensing,
  language coverage and actual loading need their own checks.
- **Colour and material:** a deliberate relationship and proportion of surfaces,
  ink and accents. Explain the effect in context; contrast alone is not a palette.
- **Imagery or authored graphics:** a repeatable subject and framing approach that
  contributes evidence or character. Use authorised assets; do not borrow a
  reference's identity or treat generated imagery as real customer evidence.
- **Composition and interaction:** a reading rhythm and recognisable treatment of
  choices, transitions and states. Carry the identity through the useful journey,
  rather than stopping at the hero.

Write a short ledger from product truth to design decision, expected effect and
rendered check. This is a design hypothesis, not proof that the user will like it.
Keep explicit acceptance separate from inferred choices.

## Compare positive alternatives

If the user rejected the shared layout or visual system, start with the
[identity reset](identity-reset.md). Record the baseline's reading order and
compare different content priorities, typography and control language. Two
palettes or illustration arrangements within the same page model are insufficient
evidence of a different approach to that rejection.

When the identity is unresolved and the redesign substantial, render two small,
materially different directions with the same facts and available assets. Compare
voice, composition and relationships as well as token values. A description of
two moods is weaker evidence than actual sketches. Do not make one candidate
deliberately poor, or compare a fully art-directed page with a wireframe.

Choose from the brief and actual renders without requiring an unnecessary user
approval gate. Keep a useful accepted direction for ordinary repairs. A brand
refresh is not a reason to remove features, replace the product or add arbitrary
sections just to make a portfolio piece.

## Recheck creation, continuity and restraint

Inspect the full page and readable details at matched desktop and phone sizes.
Name what was **created or strengthened**, not only what disappeared. Check that
the positive identity survives the service information, forms, selected choices,
errors and completion. Recheck behaviour, loading, contrast, focus and reflow.

Use a name-swap thought experiment to expose empty claims and interchangeable
decoration, while allowing conventional forms and shared components. Do not turn
distinctiveness into a novelty quota or a requirement for a mascot, unusual font,
handwriting, texture, animation or maximalism. A plain research tool, a vivid food
brand and a technical instrument can all be coherent on their own terms.

Report visual response, functional preservation and user acceptance separately.
Automated scores remain scoped to implemented checks. A clean audit, a removed
icon or an attractive hero does not establish a successful identity or parity with
a reference. Keep the existing retry and regression bounds.
