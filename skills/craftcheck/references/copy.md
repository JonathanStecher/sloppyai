# Copy, evidence and localization

Use the audience's language, supplied customer research and the actual product.
A concrete description should establish the task or benefit and relevant limits.
Place material costs and conditions before the consequential action.

Give each passage a job. Review repetition, unsupported superlatives, vague
calls to action, inconsistent terminology and unnecessary explanation. A phrase
such as seamless or Continue can be appropriate in context; do not replace words
merely because a style detector recognizes them.

Connect material claims to evidence. Never invent customers, quotes, ratings,
uptime, guarantees, timing, usage numbers, refunds or trials. Check that a citation
exists and supports the adjacent claim. Distinguish a factual assertion from a
proposal or illustrative example.

Errors should identify the problem and a safe next step without blaming users or
promising retained data that was not verified. Match tone to the user's emotional
context. Check language, text expansion, currency, dates, pluralization and
terminology across translations.

Use C catalogue entries and references/ratings.md. Static phrase checks produce
review prompts and do not determine authorship or automatically rewrite copy.

Sources: https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/design/writing-for-user-interfaces and
https://copyhackers.com/2022/06/copywriting-research/.
